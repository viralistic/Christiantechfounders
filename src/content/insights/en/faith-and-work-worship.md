# Faith and Work: What Brings Your Eyes to a Lower Place?

Faith and work connect when worship shapes the way you work. Worship lifts your eyes from the low things that steal your attention, gives God room to change you, and sends you back into your work with compassion and a sense of mission. Without it, faith and work slowly drift apart.

Most Christian founders do not decide to leave God out of their work. It just happens. A launch, a funding round, a hard month. Your attention shifts, a little at a time, until Sunday and Monday feel like two different lives.

So here is a question worth sitting with: what brings your eyes to a lower place?

## What does faith and work mean?

Faith and work means that your relationship with God and your daily work are one life, not two. The way you build, lead, sell and treat people is part of how you follow Jesus.

Paul says it plainly in Colossians 3:23: "Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord, not for human masters." Your work is not outside God's interest. It is one of the main places your faith becomes visible.

## What brings your eyes to a lower place?

Your eyes drift to a lower place when smaller things take the attention that belongs to God. For founders, those things are rarely dramatic. They are ordinary:

- Metrics you check before you pray
- Investor opinions that weigh more than God's voice
- Constant notifications that never let your mind rest
- Fear about money, reputation or failure

None of these are evil on their own. But when they become what you look at all day, your eyes are on lower things. Colossians 3:2 says: "Set your minds on things above, not on earthly things."

## Worship lifts your eyes

The goal of worship is to lift your eyes and stop your attention from being stolen by low things. Hebrews 12:2 describes the Christian life as "fixing our eyes on Jesus."

When you worship, you fix your eyes on what is faithful and loyal. God does not change with the market. He does not drop you after a bad quarter. Remembering that changes how you carry your work.

## Worship is more than feeling good

Worship feels good, and that is a gift. But worship is not mainly about feeling good. In worship we let God change us. And when He changes us, something happens:

1. **Worship leads to compassion.** Seeing God more clearly helps you see people the way He does.
2. **Compassion leads to mission.** You start to care about the people your product touches, not just the numbers.
3. **Mission leads to serving.** Your work becomes a way to serve, not only a way to win.

That is how worship and work connect. Worship is not a break from your work. It is what makes your work faithful.

## How can founders bring worship into their work week?

Founders can bring worship into their work week with small, repeatable habits that lift their eyes before the day pulls them down. Some ideas:

- **Worship before your inbox.** Start the day with a song, a psalm or a short prayer before you open email or Slack.
- **Pray over decisions.** Before a big product or hiring decision, stop and ask God for wisdom, alone or with your co-founder.
- **Name your low places.** Notice what keeps pulling your eyes down and bring it to God honestly.
- **Worship together.** Join other founders regularly. At Christian Tech Founders we meet on Sunday evenings to worship, pray and talk about calling.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I integrate faith and work as a founder?

Start with worship and prayer before work, make decisions with God in view, and treat people as made in His image. Find other Christian founders to share the journey with. Integration grows through habits, not one big decision.

### Is my work worship?

Work can be worship when you do it for God, with integrity and love for others. Colossians 3:23 calls us to work "as working for the Lord." That turns ordinary tasks into acts of devotion.

### Why does worship matter for my business?

Worship keeps your eyes on God instead of on lower things, which protects your heart and your decisions. It also moves you to compassion and mission, which changes what you build and who you build it for.
