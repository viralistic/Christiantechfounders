# Finding Your Purpose in God: A Guide for People Who Build

Your purpose in God is to know Him and to reflect Him to the world. You live that purpose out by standing where your talents fit and using them to serve others. For founders, purpose is less about a job title and more about a posture and a place.

Most founders we meet are not short on options. They are short on clarity. You could start ten companies, join three startups or go back to a safe job. All of them could be good. So the question is not "can I do something?" but "where does God want what He gave me?"

This guide walks through how we think about finding your purpose in God, based on Scripture and on many honest conversations with Christian founders.

## What is your purpose in God?

Your purpose in God has two layers: a shared purpose every believer has, and a personal purpose shaped by your talents and story.

The shared purpose is simple and old. The Westminster Shorter Catechism puts it in one line: "Man's chief end is to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever." Before you build anything, you are made to know God and enjoy Him.

The personal purpose comes next. Paul writes in Ephesians 2:10: "For we are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do." Those good works are specific. They fit the way God made you.

## Why do founders struggle to find their purpose?

Founders struggle to find their purpose because they have too many options and too much noise. When you can build almost anything, every idea looks like a possible calling.

Three things make it harder:

1. **Your eyes drift low.** Metrics, investors, cash flow and notifications quietly take the place of God in your attention.
2. **You compare.** Someone else's success story starts to feel like the purpose you are supposed to have.
3. **You decide alone.** Without people who share your faith and understand your work, the big questions stay in your head.

None of this means you are failing. It means you need a better way to look.

## How do you find your purpose in God?

You find your purpose in God by starting with worship, looking honestly at your talents, asking where they serve others most, and testing your conclusions in community.

### 1. Start with worship, not strategy

Strategy asks "what is the biggest opportunity?" Worship asks "who is God and what does He care about?" When you start with worship, your eyes lift from the low things that steal your attention. Your plans get smaller in your own eyes and God gets bigger. That is the right order.

### 2. Look honestly at your talents

Purpose rarely ignores the way God made you. Ask yourself: what do people ask me for help with? What do I do well without trying hard? What gives me energy instead of draining it? Write the answers down. They are clues.

### 3. Ask where your talents serve others most

There are thousands of things you could do. We believe a calling from God means standing in the place that lines up as closely as possible with your talents. When you stand there, you shine His light like a kaleidoscope, onto as many people as possible, as strongly as possible.

A chef can carry God into a dinner. A singer can carry a room into worship. If you love technology, your biggest impact probably comes from building with the skills and passion God gave you, not from copying someone else's calling.

### 4. Test it in community

Your own heart is not always a reliable narrator. Share what you are seeing with believers who know you and understand business. Let them pray with you and push back. Purpose that survives honest friends is usually purpose worth pursuing.

## What does purpose in God look like for founders in tech?

For founders in tech, purpose in God often looks like building products that serve people well, in a world where a few giant companies shape most of the technology we use.

That does not mean every Christian founder must build a "Christian app." It means asking faith-shaped questions in normal work. Who does this product serve? Does it treat people as made in the image of God? Would I be glad to show this to Jesus? Christians can claim more space for God's presence in technology, one decision at a time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know my purpose from God?

Look for the overlap between three things: what God says in Scripture, the talents He gave you, and the real needs around you. Then test what you see with mature believers. Clarity usually comes step by step, not all at once.

### Can my business be my purpose?

Your business can be part of your purpose, but it is not the whole of it. Your first purpose is to know and glorify God. A business becomes part of your calling when it uses your talents to serve others and honors Him in how it runs.

### What if I chose the wrong path?

God wastes very little. Skills you built on a "wrong" path often become tools for the right one. Bring it to God honestly, talk it through with others, and take the next faithful step.
